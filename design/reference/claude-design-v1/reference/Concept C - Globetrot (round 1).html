Concept C · Direction 1c

The Passport Club

Brand proposal: Globetrot. Travel planning as a collection you’re proud of. Every real step — a route drawn, a budget set, a first R$1,000 saved — earns a stamp in your trip’s passport. Bold airline-poster colour, chunky tactile controls, and Pip front and centre as the friend who’s cheering you on.

Typography

Bricolage Grotesque — one expressive family across optical size and width: tight 80% width at 800 for posters and stamps, 100% at 400 for reading. Space Mono for ticket codes, dates and money — the airline-ticket voice.

Colour

Airmail blue, stamp red, marigold, on warm cream; mint marks “done”. The map is a travel poster: blue sea, cream land, sky-tinted neighbours. Dark mode = “red-eye flight”: deep indigo, cream type, the same stamp colours slightly lifted.

Layout

Generous radii (14–28px), thick 2.5–3px ink outlines and solid offset shadows: every control looks pressable. Asymmetric poster compositions; tickets and stamps break the grid on purpose, but only one per view.

Personality

Warm, encouraging, a bit cheeky — never childish. Celebrates real progress loudly and stays out of the way when you’re working.

Motion

  • Stamps slam (scale 1.8→1, 450ms) with a soft thud + haptic.
  • Pins hop in with overshoot.
  • Routes are airmail stripes that slowly march.
  • Buttons physically press down 3px.

The companion

Pip at full volume: a cobalt globe in a red bucket hat with a sticker-covered marigold case. Has a small set of poses (wave, research, pack, fly, stamp) and appears at moments that matter.

Strengths

  • Most memorable and shareable; stamps make progress tangible.
  • Best onboarding energy for first-timers.
  • Mascot-ready brand; strong social assets.

Risks & tradeoffs

  • Hardest to keep “premium”; can tip into childish.
  • Loud colour competes with dense planning data.
  • Mascot animation budget (Rive) is a real cost.
