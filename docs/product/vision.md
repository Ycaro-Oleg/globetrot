# Product vision

## One line

Globetrot turns travel dreams into organized, achievable trips. It should feel like a personal travel universe, not a booking site, a spreadsheet or an admin dashboard.

## Lifecycle

**Dream → Discover → Plan → Save → Unlock → Explore → Remember → Share**

## Audience

- **Primary: beginner travellers.** They want to travel but don't know where to start, what things cost, how to build a route or how to save for it. They need guidance and encouragement.
- **Secondary: experienced travellers.** They plan complex multi-destination trips, track expenses in detail, prefer manual control, and enjoy recording and sharing trips. Depth must never be sacrificed for approachability.

## What makes it different (in priority order)

1. **The map board.** An interactive world map used as a workspace (corkboard, vision board, atlas), not decoration. Pins, airmail routes, destination cards, a geographic hierarchy (country → region → city → place).
2. **"Can I afford this, and when?"** A budget with honest status marks (estimate / confirmed / paid / unknown) and a savings plan answered in one sentence.
3. **Pip, the optional AI companion.** Proposes structured change lists that the user previews, edits and applies. It never takes control.
4. **Stamps and passport.** Real milestones become stamps, a decorative and collectible travel identity.
5. **Social and ranks (later).** A traveller network and a 100-rank progression that rewards real, useful activity, and is reachable for people who can't travel often.

## Product principles

1. The map is the workspace, but every map action has a non-map path.
2. Answer first, numbers second.
3. Draft before commitment: AI and destructive actions are previewable and undoable.
4. Nothing is locked. Progress celebrates; it doesn't gate.
5. Honesty: an estimate is not a price, financial readiness is not travel readiness, and a self-declared visit is not a verified one.
6. Progressive disclosure: approachable first, deep when needed.
7. Global-first: language, location, nationality and currency are independent.
8. Desktop and mobile are equally important, each with interaction patterns that suit the device.

## Assumptions

- No bookings, live prices or availability in the MVP. Prices are typical-range estimates.
- Globetrot does not hold or move user funds.
- The first release is a responsive web app/PWA. Native apps (Expo) come after the MVP.

## Key risks

- Map usability on phones.
- Scope creep (social, ranks or MCP before the core is validated).
- AI cost.
- Brazilian invoicing (NFS-e) and international payment capabilities.
- Moderation once social features exist.
- Tile and geocoding licensing.
